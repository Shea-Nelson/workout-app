Pages: a home for work you come back to

Good morning, Shea. This week: Pages — put meal plans, dashboards, and explainers somewhere you can reopen, instead of burying them in chat.

This week’s lesson: Pages, a home for work you come back to

Skills decide how a Bot works. Routines decide when. Pages decide where the finished result lives. A page is a report, memo, dashboard, tracker, or explainer your Bot builds in chat: text, tables, charts, and filters you can reopen from the Library or a link.

Official help is clear on a few points that matter at home. Ask for a page in chat and the Bot posts a card. Filters and switches on the page only change what you see; they do not save edits. To change the page itself, ask the Bot. A new page starts private; you share it with the share control (team, invitees, or anyone with the link). Public links open on pages.grok.bot with no sign-in, so keep private family details off those.

  1. Say what the page is for

    Name the job and what should be on it. Example: a keto week dinner board with nights, sides for the kids, and a grocery checklist.

  2. Open the card, keep chatting beside it

    On desktop the page fills the window and chat stays in a side panel. On iPhone, tap the card. Ask for tweaks while you look at it.

  3. Tell the Bot what to change

    Controls on the page are view-only. Say things like “Move pizza to Monday and push chicken to Tuesday” so it edits the same page.

  4. Pin or share only when it helps

    Pin meal plans and the usage dashboard in the Library. Share a public dinner recipe page to Discord if you want; keep health trackers private.

  5. Reuse the same page next run

    For weekly work (meal plan, usage report, this lesson), ask the Bot to refresh the existing page so the link stays the same.

Copy and try 
Refresh our keto meal plan Page for this week. Keep the same Page and link. Layout: one row per night Mon–Sat with the main dish, a note for kids' sides, and a short grocery list under it. No health numbers. When it's ready, give me only the link and one line on what changed.

When something should go to Nicole, put the shareable facts on a page (or a short email draft for Giles) instead of pasting a long chat dump. Her Bot Giles can open a link; you still approve any send.

Tip of the week

A new routine does not run the moment you save it. If you set “every day at 8 AM” at 8:05, the first run is tomorrow at 8. Open the routine and hit Test to try it now — and use a safe example, because a test does real work. Keep anything that messages Nicole, posts to Discord, or spends money behind your OK.
